# Neutron Star

**Summary**: The ultra-dense remnants of massive stars that have collapsed after a supernova explosion, governed by the physics of degenerate matter and extreme gravitation.

**Sources**: [[pulsars.md]], [[s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf]], [[stac3719.pdf]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

Neutron stars are the progenitors of [[pulsar]]s and represent the densest form of observable matter in the universe. They form when the core of a massive star (6–10 $M_{\odot}$) collapses, and the gravitational pressure overcomes electron degeneracy pressure, resulting in a core composed primarily of neutrons (source: pulsars.md).

## Internal Structure
- **Atmosphere and Crust**: A thin iron-rich atmosphere above a solid crust of nuclei and degenerate electrons.
- **Outer Core**: Composed of neutron-rich nuclei and a "superfluid" of neutrons.
- **Inner Core**: The state of matter here remains one of the great mysteries of modern physics. It may contain hyperons, deconfined quarks, or meson condensates (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## The Equation of State (EoS)
The EoS describes the relationship between pressure and density in the neutron star interior. 
- **Soft EoS**: Predicts that matter is relatively compressible, leading to smaller maximum masses for neutron stars.
- **Stiff EoS**: Predicts that matter is less compressible, allowing for higher maximum masses.
- **Constraints**: The discovery of high-mass pulsars like [[psr-j0740+6620]] ($2.14 M_{\odot}$) provides a "stiffness" floor for EoS models, ruling out soft EoS versions that cannot support such gravity without collapsing into a black hole (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## Physical Properties
- **Density**: A teaspoon of neutron star material would weigh approximately 1 billion tons.
- **Magnetism**: Conservation of magnetic flux during collapse results in fields ranging from $10^{8}$ G (in [[millisecond-pulsar]]s) to over $10^{13}$ G in young pulsars (source: pulsar-info.txt).
- **Rotation**: Conservation of angular momentum leads to spin periods as fast as 1.4 ms.
