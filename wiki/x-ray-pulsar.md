# X-ray Pulsar

**Summary**: Pulsars that emit X-rays, typically powered by the accretion of material from a companion star in a binary system.

**Sources**: [[pulsars.md]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

X-ray pulsars are the precursors to [[millisecond-pulsar]]s. They are binary systems where a [[neutron-star]] captures matter from a donor companion.

## Accretion Mechanisms
1. **Roche-Lobe Overflow**: The companion star fills its Roche lobe, and matter flows through the inner Lagrangian point into an accretion disk. This is typical of **Low-Mass X-ray Binaries (LMXBs)**.
2. **Stellar Wind Accretion**: The neutron star captures matter from the intense stellar wind of a massive companion. This is typical of **High-Mass X-ray Binaries (HMXBs)** (source: pulsars.md).

## Emission Physics
As the accreted matter falls toward the neutron star, it is channeled by the intense magnetic field ($10^{12}$ G) onto the magnetic poles. This creates "hot spots" that emit X-rays. As the star rotates, these hot spots appear as periodic X-ray pulses.

## Torque and Spin Evolution
- **Spin-up**: In LMXBs, the accretion disk exerts a positive torque, spinning the pulsar up. This is the process that creates [[millisecond-pulsar]]s.
- **Spin-down**: In some HMXBs, the interaction between the magnetosphere and the incoming wind can exert a negative torque, slowing the pulsar down (source: pulsars.md).
