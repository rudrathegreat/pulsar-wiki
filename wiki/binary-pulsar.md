# Binary Pulsar

**Summary**: Pulsars located in orbit around a companion star, providing laboratories for orbital dynamics, stellar evolution, and relativistic physics.

**Sources**: [[pulsars.md]], [[stac3719.pdf]], [[s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

Over 10% of known pulsars are in binary systems. These systems are classified primarily by the nature of their companion (source: pulsars.md).

## Companion Types
1. **White Dwarf (WD)**: The most common companion for [[millisecond-pulsar]]s. These systems are typically circular due to tidal forces during the mass-transfer (recycling) phase (source: stac3719.pdf).
2. **Neutron Star (NS)**: Double neutron star systems (e.g., the Hulse-Taylor pulsar) are rare and provide the best tests of gravitational wave emission.
3. **Main Sequence (MS) Stars**: Rare, often found in eccentric orbits where the pulsar interacts with the companion's stellar wind.

## Orbital Dynamics
The motion of the pulsar in its orbit adds periodic shifts to the pulse arrival times.
- **Keplerian Parameters**: Period ($P_b$), eccentricity ($e$), semi-major axis ($x$), longitude of periastron ($\omega$), and time of periastron passage ($T_0$).
- **Post-Keplerian (PK) Parameters**: Relativistic corrections required for high-precision timing, including [[shapiro-delay]], periastron advance ($\dot{\omega}$), and orbital decay ($\dot{P}_b$) (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## The Recycling Process
Most binary pulsars have undergone a phase of mass transfer from the companion.
- **Spin-up**: As matter is accreted, the pulsar gains angular momentum, spinning up to millisecond periods.
- **Magnetic Field Decay**: The accretion process is thought to "bury" or decay the pulsar's magnetic field, leading to the low B-fields observed in [[millisecond-pulsar]]s (source: pulsars.md).
