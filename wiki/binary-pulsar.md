# Binary Pulsar

**Summary**: Pulsars located in orbit around a companion star, providing laboratories for orbital dynamics, stellar evolution, and relativistic physics.

**Sources**: [pulsars.md](/#file/pulsars.md), [stac3719.pdf](/#file/stac3719.pdf), [s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf](/#file/s41550-019-0880-2%20%281%29.pdf), [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-23

---

Over 10% of known pulsars are in binary systems. These systems are classified primarily by the nature of their companion (source: [pulsars.md](/#file/pulsars.md)).

## Companion Types
1. **White Dwarf (WD)**: The most common companion for [[millisecond-pulsar]]s. These systems are typically circular due to tidal forces during the mass-transfer (recycling) phase (source: [stac3719.pdf](/#file/stac3719.pdf)).
2. **Neutron Star (NS)**: Double neutron star systems, including [[psr-b1913-16]], are rare and provide tests of gravitational-radiation emission and relativistic orbital dynamics (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
3. **Main Sequence (MS) Stars**: Rare, often found in eccentric orbits where the pulsar interacts with the companion's stellar wind.

## Orbital Dynamics
The motion of the pulsar in its orbit adds periodic shifts to the pulse arrival times.
- **Keplerian Parameters**: Period ($P_b$), eccentricity ($e$), semi-major axis ($x$), longitude of periastron ($\omega$), and time of periastron passage ($T_0$).
- **Post-Keplerian (PK) Parameters**: Relativistic corrections required for high-precision timing, including [[shapiro-delay]], periastron advance, gravitational redshift/time dilation, and orbital decay (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf); [s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf](/#file/s41550-019-0880-2%20%281%29.pdf)).

For PSR B1913+16, the PK parameters yield precise component masses and an orbital-decay test of general relativity (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

## The Recycling Process
Most binary pulsars have undergone a phase of mass transfer from the companion.
- **Spin-up**: As matter is accreted, the pulsar gains angular momentum, spinning up to millisecond periods.
- **Magnetic Field Decay**: The accretion process is thought to "bury" or decay the pulsar's magnetic field, leading to the low B-fields observed in [[millisecond-pulsar]]s (source: [pulsars.md](/#file/pulsars.md)).
