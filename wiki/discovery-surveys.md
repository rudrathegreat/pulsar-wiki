# Discovery Surveys

**Summary**: Large-scale systematic searches of the sky that have identified the over 3,000 pulsars known today.

**Sources**: [[pulsar-info.txt]], [[papers.txt]], [[stac3719.pdf]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

Systematic surveys are the lifeblood of pulsar astronomy. They use specialized search pipelines (e.g., PRESTO) to find periodic signals in massive radio or gamma-ray datasets.

## Major Radio Surveys
- **Parkes Multibeam Pulsar Survey (PMPS)**: Conducted at 1.4 GHz, it doubled the number of known pulsars and discovered many young sources in the Galactic plane.
- **Green Bank Northern Celestial Cap (GBNCC)**: A 350 MHz survey designed to find pulsars in the Northern sky. It discovered [[psr-j0740+6620]] (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).
- **PALFA Survey**: A deep survey of the Galactic plane using the Arecibo ALFA multibeam receiver. It is particularly sensitive to distant and highly dispersed pulsars.
- **FAST UWB Survey**: Using the massive 500m FAST telescope in China, this survey has recently discovered hundreds of new pulsars (source: papers.txt).
- **MeerKAT TRAPUM**: A survey targeting specific regions like globular clusters and supernova remnants. It recently discovered several new MSPs in **47Tuc** (source: pulsar-info.txt).

## Gamma-ray Surveys
- **Fermi LAT Blind Search**: By analyzing photon arrival times from the Fermi Large Area Telescope, astronomers have found dozens of "radio-quiet" pulsars like **J0007+7303** (source: pulsar-info.txt).
- **Fermi Targeted Searches**: Radio telescopes like the GBT and MeerKAT search "unassociated" Fermi sources, leading to the discovery of many new [[millisecond-pulsar]]s (source: papers.txt).

## Survey Significance
Different surveys are sensitive to different populations:
- **Low-frequency surveys (e.g., GBNCC)**: Good for finding pulsars with steep spectra.
- **High-frequency surveys (e.g., PMPS)**: Better for finding pulsars deep in the Galactic plane where dispersion is high.
