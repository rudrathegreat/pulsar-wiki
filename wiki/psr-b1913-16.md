# PSR B1913+16

**Summary**: The Hulse–Taylor pulsar, a relativistic double-neutron-star binary whose precision timing provides a stringent test of general relativity and gravitational-radiation damping.

**Sources**: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-23

---

PSR B1913+16 is a radio pulsar in an eccentric binary with another neutron star. Its long timing baseline enables measurements of [[post-keplerian-parameters]], component masses, orbital decay, astrometry, and relativistic spin-precession effects (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

The timing solution gives a total mass of \(2.828378\pm0.000007\,M_\odot\), with pulsar and companion masses of \(1.4398\pm0.0002\,M_\odot\) and \(1.3886\pm0.0002\,M_\odot\), respectively (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

After correcting for Galactic kinematic effects, the measured orbital-period decay agrees with the general-relativistic prediction to \(0.997\pm0.002\). This is evidence for gravitational-radiation damping in the binary, not a direct gravitational-wave detection (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

The system has a measured proper motion of \((-1.43,-0.70)\) mas yr\(^{-1}\) in right ascension and declination, respectively. The paper also reports a confirmed 2003 glitch and discusses a possible but unconfirmed 1992 timing event (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
