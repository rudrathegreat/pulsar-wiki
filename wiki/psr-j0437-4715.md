# PSR J0437-4715

**Summary**: The closest and brightest millisecond pulsar known, serving as a primary laboratory for high-precision timing and relativistic physics.

**Sources**: [[psrchive.pdf]], [[tempo2_manual.pdf]], [[stac3719.pdf]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

PSR J0437-4715 is a benchmark object in pulsar astronomy. It was the first [[millisecond-pulsar]] discovered in the Southern sky (Parkes 70-cm survey) and remains one of the most important sources for the PPTA (Parkes Pulsar Timing Array).

## Physical Properties
- **Spin Period**: 5.75 milliseconds.
- **Binary System**: It is in a 5.7-day orbit with a white dwarf companion (source: tempo2_manual.pdf).
- **Proximity**: Located just 156 pc away.

## Timing Significance
Due to its extreme brightness and sharp pulse profile, J0437-4715 can be timed with nanosecond precision. It is used to:
- Test theories of gravity.
- Search for the stochastic background of gravitational waves in a [[pulsar-timing-array]].
- Study high-cadence [[dispersion-measure]] variations (source: stac3719.pdf).

## Data Analysis Laboratory
Many of the fundamental algorithms in [[psrchive]] and [[tempo2]] were refined using datasets from this pulsar. Its complex profile requires rigorous **Polarimetric Calibration** to achieve the highest timing accuracy (source: psrchive.pdf).
