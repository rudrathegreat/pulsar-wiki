# Millisecond Pulsar

**Summary**: Extremely rapidly rotating pulsars ($P < 50$ ms) with low magnetic fields, typically formed through a "recycling" process in binary systems.

**Sources**: [[pulsars.md]], [[pulsar-info.txt]], [[s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf]], [[stac3719.pdf]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

Millisecond pulsars (MSPs) are the most stable clocks in the universe. They are "recycled" pulsars that have been spun up to high frequencies by accreting matter from a companion star (source: pulsars.md).

## Formation and Evolution
1. **Pulsar Birth**: A young pulsar is born in a binary system.
2. **Death Line**: It eventually slows down and ceases radio emission.
3. **Accretion Phase**: The companion star evolves and begins transferring mass (visible as an [[x-ray-pulsar]] LMXB).
4. **Recycling**: The angular momentum of the accreted matter "spins up" the neutron star to millisecond periods.
5. **MSP Phase**: Accretion ends, and the star resumes life as a radio-emitting MSP with a very low magnetic field ($10^8$–$10^9$ G) (source: pulsars.md).

## Precision Timing and Gravitational Waves
Due to their extreme rotational stability, MSPs are the primary components of **Pulsar Timing Arrays (PTAs)**. By monitoring dozens of MSPs, astronomers search for the characteristic correlated residuals caused by a stochastic background of low-frequency gravitational waves (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## Mass and EoS Constraints
MSPs often exist in systems where [[shapiro-delay]] can be measured.
- **Record Mass**: [[psr-j0740+6620]] is the most massive MSP known at $2.14 M_{\odot}$.
- **Significance**: These high masses provide a laboratory for nuclear physics, testing the behavior of matter at densities exceeding that of atomic nuclei (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## Notable Catalog Entries
- **J0024-7204aa**: A very fast MSP (1.84 ms) in the globular cluster 47Tuc.
- **J0023+0923**: An ancient, stable MSP with a 3.05 ms period (source: pulsar-info.txt).
