# Pulsar Glitches

**Summary**: Pulsar glitches are abrupt increases in a pulsar’s rotation rate. They are identified through precision timing and provide a way to investigate the otherwise inaccessible interior of a [[neutron-star]].

**Sources**: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt), [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-23

---

## What is a glitch?

A pulsar normally spins down gradually as it loses rotational energy. A **glitch** is a sudden increase in its rotation rate, so the pulsar briefly spins faster than predicted by a smooth spin-down model (source: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt)).

Because the spin frequency and rotation period are inversely related, an increase in spin frequency corresponds to a decrease in the rotation period (source: [pulsars.md](/#file/pulsars.md)). In timing data, the event appears as a sudden change in the predicted pulse phase or in the measured pulse arrival times, followed by a revised timing model (source: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt)).

## PSR B1913+16 example

The long timing record contains a confirmed May 2003 glitch with \(\Delta f/f=3.7\times10^{-11}\). The paper also discusses a possible mid-1992 timing event, but does not treat it as secure because higher-order frequency derivatives or timing-noise models provide comparably good fits (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

The distinction illustrates why an apparent change in timing behaviour must be separated from a securely measured rotational glitch (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

## How glitches are detected

Astronomers monitor a pulsar’s [[pulsar-timing|pulse arrival times]] over many observing epochs. By accounting for the rotations between observations, they can track the pulsar’s rotational history and identify departures from the expected evolution (source: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt)).

A glitch is therefore a timing event rather than simply a change in pulse brightness. Its defining observational signature is a rapid increase in the inferred rotation rate (source: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt)).

## Why glitches matter

Glitches allow astronomers to probe the internal physics of pulsars and [[neutron-star|neutron stars]], which cannot be observed directly (source: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt)). In particular, the source material identifies the changing [[neutron-star|superfluid]] interior and its coupling to the magnetic field as proposed explanations; these should be treated as hypotheses rather than as a settled mechanism (source: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt)).

The [[vela-pulsar]] is an especially important glitch laboratory: it is described as a young pulsar with frequent, large glitches, making it a prominent target for studying the interaction between the crust and internal superfluid neutrons (source: [pulsar-basics.txt](/#file/pulsar-basics.txt); source: [pulsars.md](/#file/pulsars.md)).

## Open questions

The available wiki sources do not specify a complete physical model for the trigger, the transfer of angular momentum, or the subsequent recovery of a glitch. These topics need further verification from dedicated glitch literature. In particular, future expansion of this page should distinguish observationally established timing behaviour from competing models of crust–superfluid coupling.
