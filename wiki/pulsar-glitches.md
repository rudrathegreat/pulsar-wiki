# Pulsar Glitches

**Summary**: Pulsar glitches are abrupt increases in a pulsar’s rotation rate. They are identified through precision timing and provide a way to investigate the otherwise inaccessible interior of a [[neutron-star]].

**Sources**: pulsar-basics.txt

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

## What is a glitch?

A pulsar normally spins down gradually as it loses rotational energy. A **glitch** is a sudden increase in its rotation rate, so the pulsar briefly spins faster than predicted by a smooth spin-down model (source: pulsar-basics.txt).

Because the spin frequency and rotation period are inversely related, an increase in spin frequency corresponds to a decrease in the rotation period (source: pulsars.md). In timing data, the event appears as a sudden change in the predicted pulse phase or in the measured pulse arrival times, followed by a revised timing model (source: pulsar-basics.txt).

The term is sometimes compared with a “starquake,” but the wiki source material does not establish that crustal failure is the general cause. The underlying mechanism is described as unknown and remains a subject for verification (source: pulsar-basics.txt).

## How glitches are detected

Astronomers monitor a pulsar’s [[pulsar-timing|pulse arrival times]] over many observing epochs. By accounting for the rotations between observations, they can track the pulsar’s rotational history and identify departures from the expected evolution (source: pulsar-basics.txt).

A glitch is therefore a timing event rather than simply a change in pulse brightness. Its defining observational signature is a rapid increase in the inferred rotation rate (source: pulsar-basics.txt).

## Why glitches matter

Glitches allow astronomers to probe the internal physics of pulsars and [[neutron-star|neutron stars]], which cannot be observed directly (source: pulsar-basics.txt). In particular, the source material identifies the changing [[neutron-star|superfluid]] interior and its coupling to the magnetic field as proposed explanations; these should be treated as hypotheses rather than as a settled mechanism (source: pulsar-basics.txt).

The [[vela-pulsar]] is an especially important glitch laboratory: it is described as a young pulsar with frequent, large glitches, making it a prominent target for studying the interaction between the crust and internal superfluid neutrons (source: pulsar-basics.txt; source: pulsars.md).

## Open questions

The available wiki sources do not specify a complete physical model for the trigger, the transfer of angular momentum, or the subsequent recovery of a glitch. These topics need further verification from dedicated glitch literature. In particular, future expansion of this page should distinguish observationally established timing behaviour from competing models of crust–superfluid coupling.
