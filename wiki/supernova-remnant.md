# Supernova Remnant

**Summary**: The expanding structure resulting from a supernova explosion, which often houses a young [[pulsar]] at its center.

**Sources**: [[pulsars.md]], [[pulsar-info.txt]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

A Supernova Remnant (SNR) is the remains of a star that has exploded. For pulsar researchers, SNRs are critical because they mark the birthplaces of [[neutron-star]]s (source: pulsars.md).

## Structure
An SNR consists of ejected stellar material and interstellar gas that has been heated by the explosion's shock wave.

## Pulsar-SNR Associations
Finding a pulsar within an SNR allows astronomers to determine the pulsar's age and birth velocity accurately.
- **The Crab Nebula**: Associated with the [[crab-pulsar]], the remnant of SN 1054.
- **CTA 1**: Associated with **J0007+7303**, a pulsar discovered only through its gamma-ray emission (source: pulsar-info.txt).
- **Vela SNR**: Houses the Vela Pulsar, one of the brightest radio and gamma-ray sources in the sky.

## Pulsar Wind Nebulae (PWN)
Young pulsars often create their own structure within an SNR. The relativistic wind from the [[pulsar-magnetosphere]] interacts with the SNR gas to create a PWN, visible across many wavelengths (source: pulsars.md).
