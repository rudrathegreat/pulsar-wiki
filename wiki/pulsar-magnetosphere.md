# Pulsar Magnetosphere

**Summary**: The region surrounding a pulsar dominated by its magnetic field, where particles are accelerated to relativistic speeds to produce the observed emission.

**Sources**: [[pulsars.md]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

The pulsar magnetosphere is a complex environment where electromagnetic and plasma physics intersect. It is the "engine" that produces the pulses detected by telescopes like [[meerkat]].

## Structure
- **Light Cylinder**: The distance from the pulsar where the co-rotating speed equals the speed of light ($c$). Nothing can co-rotate beyond this point.
- **Open vs. Closed Field Lines**: Field lines that cross the light cylinder are "open" and allow particles to escape, creating the pulsar wind. Field lines that stay within the cylinder are "closed."
- **Polar Caps**: The areas on the pulsar surface where the open field lines originate. This is the primary site for particle acceleration (source: pulsars.md).

## Emission Mechanism
Particles (electrons and positrons) are accelerated along the open field lines to relativistic speeds. These particles emit **Curvature Radiation** as they follow the curved magnetic field lines.
- **Coherent Emission**: The radio emission must be coherent (particles radiating in phase) to explain the incredibly high brightness temperatures observed in [[radio-pulsar]]s (source: pulsars.md).

## The Pulsar Wind
Beyond the light cylinder, the escaping particles form a relativistic wind of plasma and magnetic fields. In some cases, this wind interacts with the surrounding environment to create a **Pulsar Wind Nebula** (PWN), such as that seen around the [[crab-pulsar]].
