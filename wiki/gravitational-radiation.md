# Gravitational-Radiation Damping

**Summary**: The loss of orbital energy and angular momentum predicted when a compact binary emits gravitational radiation, observed in PSR B1913+16 as a secular decrease in orbital period.

**Sources**: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-23

---

In an eccentric double-neutron-star binary, general relativity predicts that gravitational-radiation emission shrinks the orbit and changes the orbital period. The effect is inferred from long-term timing rather than from measuring the gravitational-wave signal directly (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

For PSR B1913+16, the observed orbital-period derivative is corrected for Galactic kinematic contributions, including the line-of-sight acceleration and the Shklovskii contribution from transverse motion. The corrected result is \(0.997\pm0.002\) of the general-relativistic prediction (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

The agreement is a strong system-specific test of general relativity. Its uncertainty is limited mainly by the distance and Galactic parameters used in the kinematic correction, rather than by the precision of the timing data (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
