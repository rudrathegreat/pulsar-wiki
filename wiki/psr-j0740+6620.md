# PSR J0740+6620

**Summary**: A millisecond pulsar that holds the record for the highest precisely measured mass of a neutron star, providing critical constraints on nuclear physics.

**Sources**: [[s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf]], [[stac3719.pdf]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

PSR J0740+6620 is a binary [[millisecond-pulsar]] that has become a cornerstone of modern astrophysics due to its extreme mass (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## Technical Profile
- **Spin Period**: 2.89 ms.
- **Orbital Period**: 4.77 days.
- **Companion**: A low-mass helium white dwarf ($M_c \approx 0.26 M_{\odot}$).
- **Binary Model**: Nearly circular orbit (eccentricity $\approx 5 \times 10^{-6}$) (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## Precise Mass Measurement
Through intense timing campaigns with the Green Bank Telescope (GBT), astronomers measured the [[shapiro-delay]] in this system. 
- **Pulsar Mass ($M_p$)**: **$2.14_{-0.09}^{+0.10} M_{\odot}$** (68.3% credibility).
- **Inclination ($i$)**: $87.38^{\circ}$ (nearly edge-on), which makes the Shapiro delay signal much stronger and easier to detect (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## Scientific Impact
The $2.14 M_{\odot}$ measurement is a "stiffness" test for the nuclear **Equation of State (EoS)**. Many theoretical models for the interior of [[neutron-star]]s (such as those involving hyperons or quark matter) predict a "soft" interior that would collapse into a black hole before reaching such a mass. PSR J0740+6620 proves that the interior matter must be "stiff" enough to support such extreme gravitation (source: s41550-019-0880-2 (1).pdf).

## Observational History
Originally discovered in the GBNCC survey, it was later added to the NANOGrav [[pulsar-timing-array]] due to its excellent timing stability.
