# Timing Measurements of the Relativistic Binary Pulsar PSR B1913+16

**Summary**: Summary of Weisberg, Huang, and Taylor's long-term timing analysis of PSR B1913+16, the Hulse–Taylor double-neutron-star system. The paper measures relativistic orbital parameters, component masses, proper motion, a confirmed glitch, and orbital decay consistent with gravitational-wave damping in general relativity.

**Sources**: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-23

---

## Dataset and timing method

The analysis uses 7,650 five-minute Arecibo pulse-profile integrations obtained between 1981 and 2006. Pulse times of arrival were measured from the profiles and fitted with TEMPO using the Damour–Deruelle relativistic binary model, the JPL DE405 Solar-System ephemeris, and an updated terrestrial time standard (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

## Main results

- The measured proper motion is \(\mu_\alpha=-1.43\pm0.13\) and \(\mu_\delta=-0.70\pm0.13\) mas yr\(^{-1}\) (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
- A confirmed May 2003 glitch has \(\Delta f/f=3.7\times10^{-11}\). A possible mid-1992 timing event is not treated as secure because higher-order frequency derivatives or timing-noise models can fit the data comparably well (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
- The total mass is \(m_1+m_2=2.828378\pm0.000007\,M_\odot\), with component masses \(m_1=1.4398\pm0.0002\,M_\odot\) and \(m_2=1.3886\pm0.0002\,M_\odot\) (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
- After the Galactic kinematic correction, the observed orbital decay agrees with the general-relativistic prediction at \((\dot P_b-\Delta\dot P_{b,\mathrm{gal}})/\dot P_b^{\mathrm{GR}}=0.997\pm0.002\). The uncertainty is dominated by the distance and Galactic-model correction (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
- The paper discusses geodetic spin precession through pulse-profile evolution and future prospects for measuring Shapiro delay as the orbital geometry changes (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
- Combining the astrometry and Galactic corrections gives approximate planar and polar velocities of 247 and 51 km s\(^{-1}\), respectively, relative to the pulsar's standard of rest (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).

The paper's orbital-decay result is a system-specific test of gravitational radiation; it should not be read as a direct detection of gravitational waves (source: [1011.0718.pdf](/#file/1011.0718.pdf)).
