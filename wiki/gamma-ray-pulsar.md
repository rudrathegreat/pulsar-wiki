# Gamma-ray Pulsar

**Summary**: Rare pulsars that emit high-energy gamma rays, often young and possessing strong magnetic fields.

**Sources**: [[pulsars.md]], [[pulsar-info.txt]]

**Last updated**: 2026-04-27

---

Gamma-ray pulsars are intense sources of high-energy radiation. The field has been revolutionized by the **Fermi Large Area Telescope (LAT)**, which has discovered a vast population of these objects through both targeted searches of radio pulsars and "blind" searches of the gamma-ray sky (source: papers.txt).

## Notable Examples & Discoveries
- **Vela Pulsar**: The strongest point source in the sky at 100 MeV.
- **Geminga**: Unique for having no detectable radio emission despite being a strong gamma-ray source.
- **J1028-5819**: A young radio pulsar discovered to emit pulsed gamma-rays by Fermi LAT (source: papers.txt).
- **J0030+0451**: A nearby [[millisecond-pulsar]] that is also a prominent gamma-ray source.
- **Blind Search Discoveries**: Fermi LAT has identified over 16 pulsars through blind frequency searches, many of which were previously unknown in the radio spectrum (source: papers.txt).

## Emission Mechanism
Gamma-ray emission is thought to originate high in the [[pulsar-magnetosphere]], in regions known as "outer gaps" or "slot gaps," where particles are accelerated to extreme energies. Unlike radio emission, gamma-ray profiles often show broad, multi-peaked structures.
